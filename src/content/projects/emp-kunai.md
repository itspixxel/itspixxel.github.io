  - title: What I took from GradEX
    text: |-
      At GradEX, the University of Staffordshire's graduate showcase, I was mainly showing ADAPTIK when industry professionals reviewed my portfolio. The feedback was blunt: if I wanted to work as a technical artist, I needed to show how I solved problems, not just put attractive effects on a page.

      I came up with the kunai as an original personal study. The brief was to make a throwable EMP ability whose animation and effects communicate what is happening: the throw, the detonation, the affected area, and the reaction of nearby targets.

      That gave me a reason for each part of the effect and something more specific to work towards than making the explosion bigger or brighter.
  - title: Timing was the main piece of work
    text: |-
      The VFX went through the most iteration. I used the study to work on animation principles, particularly anticipation, timing, arcs, and follow-through, and how those ideas carry across from the character's movement into the effect.

      The sequence combines a charging core and projectile trail with an expanding shockwave, distortion, and electric arcs at detonation. The timing needs to connect those layers to the throw so the ability reads as one action.

      The still shows the burst and its radial structure. The trailer is the better way to judge the timing: how the action builds, when the pulse lands, and how the trails follow through.

  - title: Giving the effect a gameplay purpose
    text: |-
      The pulse needed to communicate an area of effect and a disruption response. I paired the detonation with target reactions, including sparks, flickers, smoke, and particles, to make the consequence of the hit visible.

      The prototype also includes camera shake, a stunned state within the affected area, and a Blueprint interface for integration. These give the effect a place in an ability sequence rather than leaving it as a particle system running on its own.

      I treated readability as part of the brief throughout: the throw should lead into the pulse, and the target feedback should explain what the pulse did.

  - title: What this study was for
    text: |-
      EMP Kunai was a personal study in animation principles and VFX timing. The useful part was having to explain why the layers were there and how they supported the action, then keep iterating on the effect.

      It also changed what I wanted to show in my portfolio. The finished visual is one part of the project; the feedback, the brief, and the decisions behind it are worth showing too.

      Tools used: Unreal Engine 5 Niagara and Blueprints, Blender, Substance Designer, and Photoshop.

## The idea

I made EMP Kunai after feedback on ADAPTIK at [GradEX](https://www.staffs.ac.uk/gradex) pushed me to show more of my problem-solving as a technical artist. It became an original personal study of a throwable EMP ability, using Niagara VFX and character animation to explore timing and visual feedback.
